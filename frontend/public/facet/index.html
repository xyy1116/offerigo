Real-time glass, in five terms.

Iridescence — A thin-film interference effect. The surface shifts hue as the viewing angle changes, like soap or a beetle’s shell.

Index of refraction — How much light bends when it enters a material. It decides how thick, how liquid, how present the glass feels.

Dispersion — Different wavelengths bend by different amounts. That small spread is why edges throw violet and green instead of a clean white.

Transmission — Light that passes through rather than stops at the surface. It is what separates a painted dark panel from smoked glass.

Roughness — The scale of the micro-surface. Polished glass keeps reflections sharp; a rough surface turns them into a soft studio glow.
